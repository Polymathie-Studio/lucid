# LUCID: the standard

*Version 0.6.0. LUCID, Layered Understanding, Calibrated In Disclosure. The standard it implements, and how it grounds in Dimensional Frame Language.*

## What it is

A standard for placing complexity so a reader is never overwhelmed, across a whole experience and not only on a page. Tesler's law says a system's complexity cannot be deleted, only moved. LUCID moves it deliberately in two dimensions. In space, each thing is revealed at the depth the reader chooses, rather than dumped or hidden. In time, the parts of an experience are revealed in the order the reader is ready for, so the load at any one moment stays small even though the total is unchanged. The first is the mark, the atom. The second is the flow, and it is the point the standard is built toward.

## Why it exists

A website, a dashboard, a documentation surface, or a small AI tool is more and more often built by an agent working from an instruction, at a pace and volume no design review keeps up with. That work needs primitives: small, shared, dependency-free pieces a builder reaches for so the same thing is not rebuilt every time and so a sound posture holds uniformly across everything shipped, rather than being re-decided, and usually eroded, on each new surface. LUCID is one such primitive, and it has two siblings, each protecting one posture that fast building tends to drop. TEMPER protects legibility: a palette solved to contrast floors so it holds for a reader who is not the designer. LUCID protects honest disclosure: complexity placed so a reader is never walled with all of it nor floored into a false simplicity, with every claim left reachable to its basis. HASP protects privacy: an AI tool runs on the user's own key, kept in the user's browser and never sent to a server. Each answers a failure that recurs in this work: the palette that fails half its readers, the surface that dumps everything or hides what is needed, the key plumbing rebuilt insecurely on every tool. They are style guides in the working sense, not documents to consult but primitives that make the right thing the easy thing to build.

## The stance: complexity placed, not hidden

A system's complexity is conserved. It can be placed, never deleted, so the only real choice is who carries it and when. An interface can cheat on that in two opposite ways. The wall hands the reader all of it at once, so they carry everything and finish nothing. The false floor hides it to feel simple, so the reader carries nothing, understands nothing, and is quietly handed the cost of reconstructing what was hidden.

LUCID takes neither. It places the irreducible complexity on a depth axis the reader paces, hides nothing the reader might need, and forces nothing the reader cannot yet hold. The goal is not that the interface feels simple. The goal is that it stays honestly navigable at every depth. That is the line between oversimplification, which shifts complexity onto the reader behind a false floor, and honest disclosure, which layers complexity so the reader can reach all of it at their own pace. And the same stance holds in time: an experience is not dumped as a wall of steps, nor a step hidden so the whole feels short, but paced so the reader meets each part when they are ready for it.

That refusal is the first of a short set of postures the standard takes, and the set is bounded the way a well-formed design is, by naming both what it must not fall below and what it must not exceed. Three pairs, a floor and a ceiling each. On the material, the floor is that complexity stays placed, not hidden, the refusal above; the ceiling is that no level rises above the others to be the one truth, since each depth is complete at its own precision and none is a rank stood upon. On authority, between the reader and the tool, the floor is that the reader stays sovereign, keeping the pace and the depth, since the default is a default and not a mandate; the ceiling is that the tool equips the judge and never becomes one, showing the basis and stopping before the verdict. On the standard's grip on itself, the floor is that it holds firm only what protects the capability, the parts that, relaxed, would break honest disclosure; the ceiling is that it keeps a record, not a gate, so a departure from a rule is a named, scoped exception with a trail rather than a checkpoint the reader must clear. Hold the load-bearing tight and everything else loose.

## Three grains: the mark, the flow, the trail

Complexity is placed at three grains, and all three are LUCID. The mark is the atom: a single thing on a page, a term, a number, a chart, disclosed at the depth the reader chooses. Everything specified below, the three depths, the drill to source, the relations and visuals, the panel, describes the atom, and it is real work that stands on its own. But an atom placed well on one page is only half of Tesler. The other half is the flow: the whole experience placed in time, so its parts arrive in the order the reader is ready for and what they have already taken in recedes to make room for what is next. The flow is the molecule the marks compose. A reader meets it the way they meet a concept: a glance that says where they are and the one next thing, the sequence opening as they are ready, and the whole map reachable when they want it. And a third grain sits between the two. A concept stands among others it relates to, and a reader moves across those relations as surely as they descend into one concept or advance through a sequence. That movement is the trail: complexity placed in relation, disclosed by navigation. The mark places it in space, the flow in time, the trail in relation.

## The flow: complexity placed in time

A flow is an ordered set of disclosures, the steps or sections of an experience, each with a plain condition for being relevant or done. The standard does not hold the reader's state; the host supplies it, what the reader has seen, done, or dismissed, the same separation the standard already draws for a visual's data. From the flow and that state the engine yields the reader's place: the one next move, the parts behind marked done and receded, the parts ahead present but muted, and the whole reachable on demand. Calibration reads the reader's familiarity and the novelty of this particular context, so the same flow walks a newcomer and gives a returning reader the glance, and it recomputes per context rather than by a fixed label on the user. Nothing is hidden, the later parts are always reachable; the flow sets only the default order and emphasis, so the moment stays light.

## The trail: complexity placed in relation

A concept rarely stands alone. It sits among others it is broader than, narrower than, or related to, and moving across those relations is real work, distinct from descending into one concept or advancing through a sequence. That movement is the trail. Where the mark places complexity in space and the flow places it in time, the trail places it in relation; and where the depth axis is served by the panel, the relations axis is served here.

A trail is walked, not dumped. The reader stands at one concept, the concepts it relates to are offered as forks, and taking a fork moves to the chosen one and reveals it, as far as the relations run. Four things keep that navigation legible, and they hold whichever way it is laid out. Adjacency: a reveal appears next to what produced it, so cause and effect are neighbors. Persistence: every choice stays lit, so the whole chain reads at once, this led to that led to this. Direction: the chain flows one way, so its order is spatial. And propagation follows that direction, so choosing a concept refreshes everything downstream of it, not only the next step.

The reader can also stop navigating and look closer at the concept they stand on, which is the mark's own reveal opening in place, not a further fork. That is the one line to hold. Taking a fork leaves for a related concept and extends the trail; looking closer stays and deepens. Navigating among peers is the trail, deepening one thing is the mark, and conflating the two, a fork that behaves like a panel or a panel that behaves like a fork, is what makes a surface feel arbitrary.

Its parts carry plain names extended from the standard's own register. A route is one complete navigation, from where the reader enters to where it ends. A trailhead is that entry, the concept or scope a route starts from; it sits across the axis from the navigation, framing what follows rather than joining it. A leg is a stretch of a route that holds one axis, since a route may turn. A fork is a branch offered among related concepts. Trail's end is a leaf, where no fork continues, and it says so plainly, so the reader is never left wondering whether more is hidden. A map is the arrangement a trail is drawn on, the layout the designer chooses; the flow's promise that the whole map is reachable is a statement about it.

The axis belongs to the map, not to the grammar. A trail can run across the surface or down it, or turn from one into the other, and none of the four laws bends to allow the turn: which way it runs, and where the trailhead sits, are the arrangement's to choose, while that its steps stay adjacent, persistent, and directional is the grammar's to keep. And as with every surface, each control on a trail binds its text and its edges to the palette's semantic tokens, so a leaf, a lit choice, and a fork stay legible under any reader's settings rather than falling back to a default the palette cannot reach.

## The core: three readers, three depths

The structure follows the readers a substantive piece of text must serve: a general reader who can follow it, a practitioner who can use it, and a researcher who can check it. These are not three audiences to sort. They are three stations one reader can walk, in order, if they choose: to follow a claim, to use it, and to reach the basis under it. The general reader who keeps engaging becomes the practitioner; the practitioner who keeps going becomes the researcher; and becoming the researcher is arriving where you can see what the claim rests on.

| Reader | Depth | Enough to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General reader | Gloss, a one-line tooltip | follow |
| Practitioner | Explainer, a focused panel | use |
| Researcher | Detail, with a path to source | check |

This three-reader cut is a content structure, for prose. It is not asserted to govern a chart or a dataset, which have their own way of being read at overview and at detail. What spans every modality is the depth axis itself, overview to detail, of which these three prose stations are one realization.

One gesture carries all three. A concept is marked once, consistently, and the same mark opens the same depths in the same order everywhere it appears, so the reader learns the gesture once. A concept is defined once in the dictionary and read by every mark, so it means the same thing wherever it shows up.

## The deepest rung: drill to source

A compression always has a below. A number aggregates rows, a chart bins and encodes data, a gloss paraphrases a passage. The deepest rung is the reachable path from the compressed form back to that basis, so the reader can check the compression against what it came from and confirm it did not distort in the squeeze. This is a property of disclosure, not a judgment of the content: the rung shows the reader the basis and leaves the verdict with them. The tool equips the judge; it does not become the judge. A claim can also carry its own qualifications, what it assumes and where it breaks, and when it does, those are disclosed as ordinary content in the same reach, in both directions, not weighed by the standard.

## Beyond text: relations and visuals

A concept is not only its text depths. It can carry relations to other concepts, broader, narrower, and related, which are a separate axis from disclosure depth: they move between concepts, not deeper into one, so they render as their own pass, not as a depth, and that pass is the trail, where the reader navigates relations the way the panel renders depth for the mark. A concept can also carry a visual, a declarative, data-deferred spec that names its data source rather than inlining it. The module holds the spec and the model; it does not hold the instance data or a visualization runtime. The host supplies the data at render and the renderer that draws it, which keeps the module content-neutral and dependency-free, and matches the reality that one model is best served by one orchestrator across more than one renderer, a text renderer and a visualization runtime, rather than a single engine.

## Order at rest: visual hierarchy

The depth axis places a mark's complexity on demand, and the flow places an experience's in time. Neither says how the resting surface is arranged before a reader opens anything, and a surface can be confusing with nothing open, a stack of same-weight boxes where nothing leads and nothing shows what sets the terms for what. So the standard carries a hierarchy set for order at rest, a sibling to depth on demand and placement in time. Six principles, grounded in Gestalt, Nielsen Norman's visual-design work, and Refactoring UI, carried on the durable non-color devices so they hold in any palette.

1. One dominant element per view. The primary work is the center of gravity; everything else is subordinate to it. This is Gestalt figure-ground.
2. Head what reads from you, do not sit beside it. A layer whose settings the rest of the surface reads is a different order of thing, a header the content sits under, and it visibly holds what reads from it. Sitting at the top is not enough. This is Gestalt common region.
3. Treatment encodes kind. Each kind of element gets a distinct visual language; same-kind elements share it and different kinds visibly differ, which is also how peer elements within one region stay distinct. This is Gestalt similarity.
4. Emphasize by de-emphasizing. Quiet the secondary rather than shout the primary; chrome recedes and scaffolding calibrates to the reader.
5. Separate by space and elevation, not borders. Whitespace and a subtle elevation group and separate more cleanly than a stack of bordered boxes; proximity alone groups, so reserve borders for a genuine edge. This is Gestalt proximity.
6. Carry the load on non-color devices, and let color do real work. The dominance rides on structure, space, size, weight, elevation, and placement, so it holds even where color is reduced or a reader has flattened the palette. Color, through the palette's semantic tokens, is a full participant on top of that: surfaces zone the layers, semantic hues carry status, and the accent marks the one thing that needs the eye. Color deliberately, never color alone.

The sixth principle is why the hierarchy set composes with a reader-tuned palette rather than fighting it. Where the palette is reader-tuned and floored for legibility, as TEMPER's is, a hierarchy that rode on color alone would break under the reader's own settings. Carried on the non-color devices first, with color supporting through the tokens, the order holds through every mode and every reader.

## Specialist grammars

The depth axis, the flow, and the hierarchy set are general: they hold for any surface. But some surfaces belong to a kind that carries conventions of its own, hard-won and specific, that a general standard cannot supply. A pipeline is not a dashboard is not a form. So the standard carries a registry of specialist grammars, one per interface archetype, each a named set of conventions that applies only where a surface is of that kind.

A well-formed grammar does three things. It layers on the general sets and never contradicts them. Where it can, it specializes the standard's own principles into its domain rather than importing foreign ones, which is the test of coherence: a dashboard's summary-context-detail is the three depths, a conversational interface's cite-the-claim-and-open-the-reasoning is drill to source, a form's steps are the flow. And a grammar lives in this registry, not in any one product, so every consumer inherits the same one rather than reinventing it.

A product does not hold grammars; it inherits a combination. It takes the general sets always, then the specialist grammars its surfaces need, declaring which and mapping them to its screens. The product stays thin: it inherits and combines, it does not redefine. This is the same few-drivers-many-processes shape the module family already uses, applied to interface convention.

The registry, opened here and extended as new archetypes are met:

- Pipeline. A board is a diagnostic, not a prettier list, surfacing where items pile up and what has gone stale; cards decide at a glance and carry aging; a record is a figure with its timeline and linked records around it; the same objects are seen as board, table, and record.
- Metric and dashboard. Summary, then context, then detail on drill; no more than five to seven metrics per view; the most important where the eye lands; built for the glance-check, not deep analysis.
- Data table. Sort, filter, and search at the top; inline edit with an explicit save and cancel; density under the reader's control; interactions revealed on hover rather than all at once.
- Form. Validate at the field on completion, not at submit; short steps with a visible progress indicator; conditional logic that adapts to prior answers; save and resume.
- Data and financial visualization. Every metric carries a target, a prior period, or a benchmark, with reference lines for thresholds; chart by intent; no decoration competing with the data.
- Conversational and AI. Guide one turn at a time; disclose the machine and its limits; make confidence visible before the reader acts; cite the claim and open the reasoning; the person controls their own data.
- Graph and network. The failure mode is the hairball, the dense tangle a node-link diagram collapses into when too many nodes and edges overlap into crossing, unreadable clutter, so the grammar is built to avoid it. It opens on an aggregated or filtered overview, never the whole network at once, and reveals more on demand, which is the three depths as overview, then focus, then detail. Selecting a node makes it and its neighbors the figure and dims the rest, figure-ground applied by interaction. Importance rides on node size and color drawn from a centrality or contribution measure, so the central few are found without reading every label, color doing real work but never alone. Layout minimizes edge crossings and prefers continuous curved paths to polylines, since a reader follows a curve more easily than a bent line. Search, a filter, and a density control cut the graph to the part the reader needs. The idiom matches the density and the task: node-link for a sparse graph or path-following, an adjacency matrix or a ranked list when the graph is dense and the task is pattern or comparison, because node-link past a certain density is a hairball no styling saves. And every node and edge drills to its source, the interactions or contributions that produced it, so the topology is a reading to weigh rather than a verdict; a contribution or influence graph states its warrant and never scores a person behind their back.

Each entry is a seed, filled as a consumer needs it. Candidate archetypes not yet drawn: command and search, calendar and scheduling, and rich text.

## Principles

1. Place, do not delete. Complexity moves along the depth axis; it is not removed.
2. Layer, do not hide. No wall, no false floor. Hide nothing the reader might need; force nothing they cannot hold.
3. Reader-paced depth. The reader sets the level. The interface does not decide for them how much they can take.
4. Three depths, three readers, in content. Gloss, explainer, detail, always in the same order. Depth itself is the axis that spans every modality; the three-reader naming is its prose form.
5. Define once, reach anywhere. A concept lives in one dictionary and every mark reads that one entry. The mechanism is content-neutral; the dictionary a site fills in is not, and does not claim to be.
6. Drillable to source. Nothing is asserted without a reachable path to its basis; every compressed claim opens to what it rests on, so the reader can check the compression is faithful.
7. No summit. A reader goes further into one concept, never climbs to a rank above another. The gloss is the whole read at gloss precision, not a lesser truth.
8. Consistent and persistent. One mark, one gesture, the same everywhere, learned once.
9. Place in time, not only in space. A page is placed at the depth the reader chooses; an experience is placed in the order the reader is ready for, calibrated to how far along they are, with everything still reachable. The mark is the atom, the flow is the whole.
10. Order at rest, not only on demand. Before anything opens, the surface is arranged so what leads leads and a layer the rest reads from contains it rather than sitting beside it, the hierarchy carried on the non-color devices so it holds in any palette.
11. Inherit grammars, do not reinvent them. A kind of surface carries conventions of its own; those live in one registry, and a product inherits the general sets plus a declared combination of grammars and stays thin.

## The four spheres

The eleven above are the standard's Declaring sphere: what it is. Three more spheres say how to work within it. Deciding is how to choose where the standard leaves a genuine choice. Composing is how its parts relate and stack. Conforming is how a build is checked to obey. The stance stated at the top sits above all four, as the postures the whole set expresses.

## Deciding

Where the standard underdetermines, these decide, and each resolves its choice by one test rather than by taste.

1. Decide the surface by action. Lead with the doable thing and make it precise; let the orientation around it read plainly. The one next move is what decides, which also settles precision against plainness: precision where the reader acts, plainness where they orient.
2. Classify by role, not appearance. When a thing's kind or place is ambiguous, fix its invariant role and let the concrete realization vary. Its naming face is the same rule: name a thing by what it does, so the name survives reuse, never by how it looks.
3. Distinguish navigating from deepening. Choosing among related peers extends a trail; opening one thing deeper is the mark's own reveal. The first adds a step to the navigation, the second stays in place, and that single test keeps them apart.
4. Reference, do not duplicate or choose. When plainness and precision compete, let the surface carry the plain name and reach its precise source by reference, rather than copying it or picking one. This is the depth cascade turned on the vocabulary itself.
5. Make reachability legible. What has more to reach shows it; what is a leaf says so. The presence of the affordance is the promise of more, so its absence is a real signal that there is none.
6. Freeze only what protects the capability. A rule is a law if relaxing it would break honest disclosure; otherwise it is a choice the designer makes. The line between the fixed and the configurable is drawn by what it protects, not by preference.
7. Hold fuzzy classifications lightly. At a genuine seam, take the label that helps people work and keep it cheap to revise, rather than forcing a crisp answer the boundary will not support.
8. Check both floors and ceilings. Name what a design must not fall below and what it must not exceed. A one-sided instrument guards one direction and calls itself complete.

## Composing

The standard's pieces stack, and the stack has the same shape at every grammar. A grammar is the fixed floor, its laws and its atomic vocabulary together. Above it, a unit is one complete whole of that grammar; an arrangement is how units and their reveals sit in space; a product is a whole surface that adopts an arrangement and inherits the general sets and the specialist grammars it needs. The general names stay plain because they are only referred to, while each grammar brings a vivid name where a tier is inhabited: the trail's unit is a route, its arrangement a map.

The load-bearing line runs between the grammar and the arrangement. The grammar is what the standard guarantees and no arrangement may break; the arrangement is what the designer chooses. Variation is always a new arrangement, never a new law, which is the same line the Deciding sphere draws by capability.

Three relations hold the stack together. Composition builds a higher tier from lower ones. Inheritance flows one direction only, downward, so the specific reads from the general and never the reverse. Override is not a break but a named, scoped exception that still points up the chain, so a grammar or an arrangement may depart from a general rule while leaving a record of the departure, never a silent contradiction.

## Conforming

The other three spheres say how to build the thing right; this one says how to check that a given build obeys. It already has two forms. For the mark, the panel-rendering rules below are conformance made concrete: a renderer either holds them or it does not. For prose, a mechanical scan for what is forbidden and what is required is the same discipline in another medium. The trail's conformance, the checks that a navigation holds adjacency, persistence, direction, and a legible bottom, is the next to be drawn.

## Rendering the panel

The panel is a reveal, so a panel that covers the thing it explains or escapes the page defeats its own purpose. Placement is therefore part of the standard, not left to each site to rediscover. A conforming renderer holds these rules:

1. Stay on the page. The panel is clamped into the viewport on both axes, with a small gutter. It never runs off an edge, whatever the mark's position, the panel's width, or the scroll offset.
2. Open into the room. The panel opens on whichever side of the mark has more space, above or below, rather than always downward into whatever sits under the mark.
3. Cap, then scroll. The panel's height is capped to the space available on the chosen side; a concept with deep content scrolls inside the panel rather than growing past the screen.
4. Never cover the mark. The panel is offset from the mark it belongs to, so the word or visual being explained stays visible while its explanation is open.
5. Recompute on change. Placement is recalculated when the viewport resizes, when the page scrolls, and when opening a deeper rung grows the panel, so the panel stays within these rules for as long as it is open.

The reference bindings, the vanilla custom element and the React binding, both implement these rules against the same `lucid-pop` classes, so the two surfaces place the panel identically.

## Where it stands against prior work

The mechanics are established, and the standard cites them rather than claiming them. Complexity is conserved: Tesler. Reveal in stages on demand: Nielsen's progressive disclosure and Shneiderman's details on demand. Route by what the reader is doing: Diataxis. Define once, reuse everywhere: single-source practice, and controlled vocabularies as in SKOS. Link a claim to its source: provenance interfaces. Reader-typed layering of accessible-to-technical is long-standing technical-writing doctrine. Independent fields converging on the same two moves, reveal-depth-on-demand and route-by-reader, is support for the design, not a claim of invention.

What LUCID holds as its own is the assembly, not the parts: reader-typed depth walked as one graduated ascent rather than sorted audiences; the modes layered in place on a single concept rather than separated into distinct documents; one uniform mark as the entry to every depth; and a single define-once dictionary bound to that mark, with the deepest rung always a reachable path to the basis. The contribution is the whole standing together, not a new mechanism inside it.

The point, though, is in the flow. Prior art stages disclosure widget by widget: a panel here, a wizard there, each local to one control. LUCID places complexity across a whole experience, calibrated to what the reader has already met and recomputed per context, with the marks as its atoms. That whole-experience, reader-calibrated placement in time, laid on top of the placement in space, is the sense in which LUCID goes past the usual response to Tesler rather than only applying it. It is one standard for both grains, which is what a page and a process being paced by the same discipline requires.

## Grounding in Dimensional Frame Language

LUCID's depth axis is what Dimensional Frame Language calls decomposition, the structural generation direction: descending into the particulars of one concept, each level carrying the whole within it. The cascade is a constitutive containment, every level readable at full precision without any other stepping back. That is why the gloss is complete at its own depth and why the seeds of the next depth are already present in the one you stand on, and it is the load-bearing tie: each depth carries the whole, so no depth is a lesser truth. Moving between depths has a cost in the reader's attention, which is why depth is paced by the reader and never poured on. No depth is a station stood upon, which the frame guards as its No-ladder rule, restated here as the No summit principle.

## Relationships

LUCID is a sibling to TEMPER and HASP as a reusable module: TEMPER tunes the palette, HASP holds the key, LUCID paces the depth. It composes with TEMPER for its visual layer.
